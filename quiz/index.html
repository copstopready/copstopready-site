The rights behind it

What these rights actually are

Your constitutional rights are the freedoms written into the U.S. Constitution — mostly the Bill of Rights — that limit what the government, including the police, can do to you. Three of them do the heavy lifting at a traffic stop. Here's what each one is, and exactly how you use it.

Prefer to listen? Hear the whole thing read aloud.
1st Amendment

Your right to record

What it isThe 1st Amendment protects free speech and a free press. Courts have long held that recording police doing their job in public is part of that protection.
How you use itKeep your phone recording from a safe spot — a pocket or a mount. You don't have to stop when you're asked, and you don't have to hand it over. Just never physically interfere with the officer.
5th Amendment

Your right to stay silent

What it isThe 5th Amendment means you can't be forced to say things that incriminate you. Past handing over your license, registration and insurance, you don't have to answer an officer's questions.
How you use itThe moment they ask anything beyond your documents — even a friendly “where you headed?” — say it out loud, every time:
“Sorry officer, I don't answer questions without a lawyer.”
4th Amendment

Your protection from searches

What it isThe 4th Amendment protects you from unreasonable searches. Without a warrant, probable cause, or your consent, an officer generally can't search your car, your phone, or your bag.
How you use itIf they ask to search anything, decline with your words — never your body — even if they search anyway. Your clear refusal is what protects you later:
“Sorry officer, I do not consent to any searches.”
At your door

It isn't just the car

CopStopReady protects you on more than the road. The same rights follow you to your own front door — and at home your strongest protection kicks in: police generally need a warrant signed by a judge to come inside.

1Make them show the warrant. You don't have to open the door — ask them to hold it to a window or slip it under, and check it names your address.
2Don't consent. “I do not consent to a search” works at your door just like it does in the car.
3Stay silent. You don't have to answer questions through the door — the 5th follows you home.
4Keep recording. Recording police doing their job at your home is protected too.

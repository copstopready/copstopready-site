KNOW YOUR RIGHTS

The line between what you must do and what you can refuse during a traffic stop — in plain English, before the officer is at your window.

⚠️
This is general information, not legal advice. Laws vary by state and every situation is different. CopStopReady helps you exercise rights you already have — it is not a lawyer. For anything serious, consult a licensed attorney.

The three that matter most

5th Amendment

The right to remain silent

You do not have to answer questions like “do you know why I stopped you?” or “where are you coming from?” Anything you say can be used against you — and you can decline politely without it being an admission of anything.

CopStopReady coaches you to say“I am pleading the 5th and invoking my right to remain silent.”
4th Amendment

The right to refuse a search

An officer may ask to search your car. Unless they have probable cause or a warrant, you can decline consent. Refusing a search is not an admission of guilt, and stating it clearly protects you later in court.

CopStopReady coaches you to say“I do not consent to any searches.”
1st Amendment

The right to record

In all 50 states you have the right to record law enforcement performing their duties in public. CopStopReady records to a locked, on-device vault the moment you tap the mic — even with the screen off. Telling the officer you are recording is your right and it tends to de-escalate the encounter.

What you provide

License, registration & insurance

When you are driving, an officer can generally ask for your license, registration, and proof of insurance — always carry the real ones. CopStopReady's Doc Wallet is an emergency backup for the day you forget them: photos of all three kept on your phone, PIN-locked and on-device, plus renewal reminders so they never quietly expire.
